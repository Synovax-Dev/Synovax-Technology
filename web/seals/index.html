Product · seals

Every read,sealed every hour

Each time an app touches your vault, it is written to your read log. At the end of every hour, all reads are folded into one fingerprint and chained to the hour before, so the log cannot be quietly rewritten.

What a seal is

  1. A readAn app lists, searches, reads, suggests, or has hidden instructions removed. The server writes a log line with a random salt.
  2. A leafEach line becomes a salted SHA-256 hash, so a seal reveals nothing about who read what.
  3. A rootAt the end of the hour, every leaf from every vault is folded into one Merkle root.
  4. A chainThe seal's chain hash covers its root and the chain hash of the seal before, back to the first one.

Changing, removing or adding one read in a sealed hour changes that hour's root, and with it every later chain hash. A period without reads makes no seal.

Check it yourself

In your read log

Every entry shows its seal. Your vault checks the entry's proof against the seal's root, in your browser.

By hand

Press Copy entry on a read, paste it into Verify, and the page rebuilds the leaf and checks the proof, with no sign-in.

The whole chain

Verify also walks the seal chain from the first seal, and your browser remembers the newest seal it saw, so a rewritten history is caught.

Copies the server cannot change

A server can set SEAL_PUBLISH_URL so every new seal is also posted somewhere else, like a webhook or a log service. The seal chain is only as strong as its witnesses: your browser, and that copy if there is one.
