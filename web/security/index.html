Security and privacy

Built so wecannot read it

SYNOVAX encrypts everything you write on your own device, with keys that only your passphrase or your 24 recovery words can unlock. This page explains how, and where the limits are.

Your keys

When you create a vault, your browser makes a random 256-bit vault key. It is never sent anywhere in readable form. It is locked (wrapped) once for each way you can open the vault:

Passphrase
PBKDF2 with SHA-256 and 600,000 rounds over your passphrase and a random 16-byte salt gives a master secret. HKDF-SHA256 splits it into a sign-in key and a key-encryption key. The second never leaves your device; it wraps your copy of the vault key.
Recovery phrase
24 words from a list of 256 (192 bits), shown once when the vault is made. HKDF-SHA256 turns them into their own sign-in key and key-encryption key, which wrap a second copy of the vault key. Using them also turns two-step sign-in off, for the day the phone with your authenticator is gone.

The server receives each sign-in key and keeps only its SHA-256 hash. A sign-in key proves who you are; it unlocks nothing. Every new vault on a server uses the same number of rounds, and the server answers the salt question with a made-up but stable salt for names that do not exist, so it does not reveal which names are registered at sign-in.

Encryption

Every folder has its own random 256-bit key, wrapped by your vault key. Every memory is stored as two parts, a head (title, tags, the first 240 characters, where it came from) and a body (the whole memory), each encrypted with its folder's key using AES-256-GCM, a fresh random 96-bit IV and the WebCrypto built into your browser. Folder names and app names are encrypted too.

Each ciphertext carries additional authenticated data naming the exact record it belongs to, such as memory:<id>:body or folder:<id>:key. A memory copied into another record, or a key moved to another folder, fails to decrypt instead of showing the wrong content. The full list is in the open standard.

The site is served with a content security policy that lets pages load scripts, styles and fonts only from SYNOVAX itself and talk only to SYNOVAX's own server. There are no analytics, no advertising and no third-party scripts, and the fonts are self-hosted.

What the server can and cannot see

It cannot see the title, text or tags of any memory, the names of your folders, the names of your apps, what an app searched for, what a suggestion says, your passphrase, your recovery words, or any of your keys.

It can see your vault name and when the vault was made; how many folders and memories there are, their sizes and when they changed; which app may read which folder; the read log (which app touched which memory id, and when); your signed-in sessions with a browser and system label; and your two-step sign-in secret, which it must read to check your codes and keeps encrypted with a key from its own secret file. This is needed to run the service. The privacy page lists every item.

Connected apps

Each app gets its own 256-bit secret, made in your browser and shown once as the app key. HKDF-SHA256 derives three things from it: a token the connector sends (the server keeps only its hash), a key that unwraps the folder keys you granted to that app, and a key for checking your vault's inbox key. Folders you did not grant are never wrapped for the app, so it holds no key that could open them, and the server refuses those requests as well.

The connector runs on your computer and decrypts there. Every request it makes carries the token, so revoking an app takes effect on its next request; the server also deletes that app's wrapped folder keys, and your vault gives those folders new keys.

Apps cannot change or delete memories. An app you allow may suggest one: the connector seals it on your computer to your vault's public key, after checking a tag your vault made over that key with the app's own secret, so a server that swapped in a key of its own would be caught before anything is sealed.

Hidden instructions in memories

Text from mail, chats and imports can carry lines written to steer an AI. Before any memory reaches a model, the connector drops invisible characters, replaces places that look like instructions for an AI with a marker, and quotes the rest as data with a code that is new for every answer. Your vault marks such memories when they come in, and every catch goes in your read log. A public test attacks these checks every hour: see the immune system.

Deleting means deleted

Deleting a memory, a folder or your account removes the rows from the database, which is set to overwrite deleted content on disk (PRAGMA secure_delete) rather than just unlink it. Deleting your account removes your sessions, folders, memories, apps, inbox and read log in one step.

The server also keeps automatic copies of its database, by default one every 6 hours and the newest 14, so deleted data can remain in those copies for up to about three and a half days. They hold the same ciphertext and hashes as the database.

Hourly seals

Every request by a connected app is written to your read log with a random 16-byte salt. The entry and its salt are hashed into a leaf: SHA-256(0x00 ‖ entry). When an hour is over, all leaves from that hour, from every vault, are combined into one Merkle root, the seal for that hour. Each seal also stores a chain hash over the seal before it, so no seal can be changed or dropped without breaking every later one.

In your vault, Verify on a log entry recomputes its leaf from the entry, fetches the proof, and checks in your browser that it leads to the published root. Because each leaf is salted and the salt is shown only to the vault's owner, the roots reveal nothing about who read what. Your vault also keeps the newest seal it has seen and checks, each time you open the read log, that the chain still leads from it. A server can post each seal to a webhook outside its control (SEAL_PUBLISH_URL), so a copy of the chain lives somewhere it cannot edit.

Sign-in and sessions

  • Only failed attempts count toward the limits, per name and address, so a stranger guessing your passphrase cannot lock you out. A cap of 200 failures an hour per vault slows a guesser spread over many addresses, and blocks only addresses that have failed on that vault.
  • A session token is 256 random bits; the server keeps its hash for 7 days. The vault page keeps the token only in the open tab's memory: no cookie, nothing in browser storage. Closing or reloading the tab locks the vault.
  • Because sign-in rides in a header, not a cookie, another site cannot send requests in your name.
  • Two-step codes are 6 digits, every code works once, and the server checks it after the passphrase.
  • Pages refuse to be framed, send no referrer, and set nosniff. When the server serves HTTPS itself (TLS_CERT), it also sends HSTS.

Limits, plainly

  • Lost keys are lost. Without the passphrase and the 24 words, nobody can open the vault.
  • A weak passphrase is the weak point. Someone with a copy of the database can try passphrases offline, at 600,000 PBKDF2 rounds per guess. Use a long one.
  • What an AI has read, it has. Revoking stops future reads, not past ones.
  • Your device is part of the system. Encryption protects the vault on the server and on the network, not from malware on the computer where you open it.
  • The page is served by the server. Like any web app with end-to-end encryption, it is only as honest as the code the server sends. The code is open source, so it can be compared, and you can run your own copy.
  • Seals cannot prove a read was written down. They prove that logged entries were not changed later.
  • Early software. It is tested on every change, and it has not had an independent audit yet.

Each of these, and what SYNOVAX does protect against, is in the threat model.

Reporting a problem

Please report a security issue privately, through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on the repository (Security, Report a vulnerability), not in a public issue. Say what you found, how to reproduce it and what someone could do with it. Do not test against vaults that are not yours. There is no bounty at present.
